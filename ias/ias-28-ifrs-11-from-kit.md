# 1. IAS 28 — الشركة الزميلة (Associate)

## 1a. From the Study Text (ST Ch 20 §1.5, p.385–386)
- Significant influence = power to participate in the investee's financial and operating policy decisions, but not control or joint control (IAS 28 para. 3, quoted at ST p.385).
- Holding 20% or more of voting power (directly or through subsidiaries) is presumed to give significant influence unless clearly shown otherwise; under 20% presumed not, unless shown otherwise (IAS 28 para. 5, ST p.385).
- Usual evidence (IAS 28 para. 6, ST p.386): board representation; participation in policy-making; material transactions with the investee; interchange of managerial personnel; provision of essential technical information.
- IAS 28 requires the equity method for associates (ST p.386). Joint control wording also appears in IAS 24 coverage (ST p.357).

## Significant influence / التأثير الجوهري
- An investor has an associate when it has significant influence but not control (Kit fp.170, Q34; fp.279, Mock 3 Q4).
- Presumption: holding **20% or more** of the equity shares gives significant influence. The presumption can be rebutted (Kit fp.170, fp.279).
- Check control first (IFRS 10). If there is no control, test for significant influence. Examples in the Kit:
  - 35% of shares + right to appoint 2 of 6 directors → associate, not subsidiary (Kit fp.170).
  - 40% of shares + right to appoint 4 of 10 directors, no evidence other holders act together against the investor → associate (Kit fp.279).

## Equity method / طريقة حقوق الملكية
- Measured at **cost**, then adjusted for the investor's **share of the post-acquisition change in the associate's net assets** (Kit fp.170, fp.279).
- The investor's share of the post-acquisition profit goes to profit or loss; its share of other comprehensive income goes to OCI (Kit fp.170).
- The carrying amount is **not** the fair value of the shares. Fair value is set by the market (Kit fp.170, fp.279).
- Required when the investor prepares consolidated financial statements (Kit fp.170).

## Worked pattern from the Kit (Kit fp.193, Q42 Alpha, Dec 2022)
Investment in associate (Delta), 25% held:

| Line | $000 |
|---|---|
| Cost | 18,000 |
| 25% × post-acquisition profits (79,000 − 70,000) | 2,250 |
| **Carrying amount** | **20,250** |

Formula: Carrying amount = Cost + investor % × (associate's net assets now − net assets at acquisition).

## Transactions with the associate / المعاملات مع الزميلة
- Profits on trading with the associate are eliminated **only to the investor's share** (Kit fp.170).
- The elimination is made **against the "investment in associate"** line, not against inventory or group profit as for a subsidiary (Kit fp.187, examiner comment, Q41).
- **No** adjustment for cash in transit from an associate, because an associate is not consolidated line by line (Kit fp.187).

## Impairment
- If the investment is impaired, write it down to its **recoverable amount** (Kit fp.170).
- Kit fp.193 (Q42) shows an equity-accounted investment alongside an impairment working for another entity; the impairment test for the associate itself is not explained there.

## Tax angle (Kit Q20 Edgworth, June 2016)
- The question states a 40% associate equity-accounted with carrying value 70m → 75m, and that the local tax only applies when profits are distributed or the investment is sold. The deferred tax answer is in Kit fp.136–139 (Q20 answer). It is an IAS 12 topic and belongs on the IAS 12 page.

## Exam traps (from examiner comments)
1. Treating an associate like a subsidiary (line-by-line, cash in transit, full unrealised-profit elimination). Kit fp.187.
2. Forgetting that only the investor's share of unrealised profit is eliminated. Kit fp.187.
3. Saying "20% = automatic associate". It is a rebuttable presumption; look at board seats and other holders. Kit fp.170, fp.279.

# 2. IFRS 11 — الترتيبات المشتركة (Joint arrangements)

## Joint control
- A joint arrangement is one where **two or more parties have joint control** (Kit fp.100; fp.256).
- Joint control = contractually agreed sharing of control; decisions about the relevant activities need the **unanimous consent** of the parties (Kit fp.256).
- Typical Kit facts that show joint control: pricing, selling and manufacturing decisions need both parties' agreement (Kit fp.100, fp.131).

## Two types
| | Joint operation | Joint venture |
|---|---|---|
| Parties have | Direct rights to assets and obligations for liabilities | Rights to the **net assets** of the arrangement |
| Typical sign | No separate entity set up; arrangement is a vehicle for running the investors' own businesses more effectively | A business basically separate from the investors' own businesses |
| Accounting | Recognise **own share** of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses | **Equity method**, single line investment |
| Kit source | fp.100, fp.131, fp.256–257 | fp.256–257 |

(Kit fp.256–257, Mock 2 Q4: investment 1 = joint operation, investment 2 = joint venture.)

## Joint operation — numbers from the Kit
- Delta/Drax, 50:50 (Kit fp.100, Q4): revenue 40m × 50% = **20m**; cost of sales 26m × 50% = **13m**, both in the operating category of the statement of profit or loss.
- Gamma/entity B, 50:50 (Kit fp.131, Q17): revenue 22m × 50% = **11m**; irrecoverable debt 100,000 × 50% = **50,000** expense.
- Mock 2 Q4 (Kit fp.257): cash invested in vehicles → Sinker shows its **share of the vehicles as PPE** and its share of running costs as expenses.

## Joint venture — equity method
- Investment measured as one amount: investor's share of the net assets, so its share of post-acquisition profit is recognised over the life of the investment (Kit fp.257).
- Same mechanics as the associate calculation above.

## Exam traps
1. Calling a no-entity, direct-rights arrangement a "joint venture". Examiners noted candidates often failed to explain that the scenario was a joint operation rather than a joint venture (Kit fp.98, examiner comments on Q4).
2. Using the equity method for a joint operation. Wrong: use line-by-line share.
3. Writing "50% of revenue" in the wrong place. In the Kit answers it sits in the operating category (IFRS 18 presentation).

# 3. Comparison / مقارنة

| | Subsidiary (IFRS 10) | Associate (IAS 28) | Joint venture (IFRS 11 + IAS 28) | Joint operation (IFRS 11) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Test | Control | Significant influence (20%+ presumed) | Joint control, rights to net assets | Joint control, direct rights/obligations |
| Method | Full consolidation | Equity method | Equity method | Share of each asset, liability, revenue, expense |
| Unrealised profit | Eliminate in full | Investor's share only, against investment | Same as associate (not stated explicitly in the Kit) | Not explained in the Kit |
| Cash in transit | Adjust | No adjustment | Not explained in the Kit | Not explained in the Kit |

Note: the equity-method treatment of a joint venture is stated in the Kit (fp.257). The IAS 28 link for a joint venture is not spelled out in the Kit beyond the title "Investments in Associates and Joint Ventures".

# 5. Quick review
- Control? → IFRS 10 subsidiary. No control but 20%+ / board influence → associate (rebuttable).
- Associate = cost + share of post-acquisition net asset change. Not fair value.
- Unrealised profit with associate: investor's share only, against the investment.
- Joint control = unanimous consent. Direct rights → joint operation → share of lines. Net assets → joint venture → equity method.
